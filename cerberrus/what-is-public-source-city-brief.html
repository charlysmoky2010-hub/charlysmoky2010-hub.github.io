What is a public-source city brief?

A public-source city brief is a bounded research snapshot that organizes publicly available situational signals around a selected city or region. Instead of presenting raw feeds, it summarizes what was observed, which sources responded, what changed, and what cannot be concluded.

Typical signal layers

Examples include public airspace observations, earthquake feeds, public-camera registries where available, satellite catalog context and launch schedules. A good brief labels global context separately from local observations.

Why not just use a dashboard?

Dashboards are useful when someone wants to explore data continuously. A brief is useful when the reader wants a compact, explainable snapshot without interpreting multiple feeds.

What a responsible brief does not claim

It does not claim exhaustive surveillance, private-person tracking, intent detection, or certainty beyond the underlying sources. It should expose scope, freshness and provenance.

CERBERRUS approach

CERBERRUS treats the brief as a research artifact: bounded area, explicit source health, provenance fingerprint and visible limitations.
